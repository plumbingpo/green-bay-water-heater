Keeping Your Ashwaubenon Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
