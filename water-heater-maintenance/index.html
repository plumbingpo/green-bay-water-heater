Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Green Bay, WI, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

The work is hands-on and specific. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Green Bay

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use

Why Professional Water Heater Maintenance Matters

Homeowners can handle the basics: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest carries more risk than it looks. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not a place to guess.

We come prepared for what an older heater throws at us: replacement drain valves, anode rods sized for your tank, flexible anodes for tight clearances, T&P valves, and meters for testing elements and thermostats. For tankless systems, we bring the pump and descaling setup. Just as important, we know what to look for, from early corrosion at the fittings to a thermocouple starting to weaken, so you can plan a repair before it becomes a failure. The goal is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
