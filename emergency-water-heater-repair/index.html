How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can spill combustion gases if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Capping that valve, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are mistakes we see far too often.

Why call a technician instead of improvising? Certainty and safe sequencing. We verify the shutoffs, test for gas leaks with real instruments, and confirm the area is safe for electrical work. Then we find the root cause. A discharging relief valve often means high street pressure or no room for thermal expansion; a puddle might be a drain valve, a dielectric fitting, condensation, or a failed tank; a cold house may trace back to a thermocouple, an element, or a tripped high-limit switch. No two of those share a fix. We repair what can honestly be repaired, and when the tank is the problem, we explain your replacement options without pressure.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
